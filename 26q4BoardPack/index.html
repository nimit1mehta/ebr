    Methodology
    • 2026 ARR (P): where we expect to end the year. Renewals we expect to keep, plus price uplift, plus upsell, expansion and new bookings (signed deals, plus open deals counted at their stage odds), plus services subscriptions held flat at $389K.
    • Bookings (P): new ARR we expect to sign in 2026. Deals already won with a 2026 close date, plus open deals closing in 2026 counted at their stage odds: B 10%, C 30%, D 60%, E 90%.
    • GRR (P): of last year's ARR up for renewal, the share we expect to keep, before any growth. Renewals already done count at what actually renewed. Open renewals count at last year's ARR, discounted by risk: Low 100%, Medium 90%, High 75%.
    • NRR (P): the same as GRR, plus growth from those customers: price uplift, upsell and expansion (signed, plus open deals at their stage odds).
    • Budget (B): fixed figures from the 2026 budget.
    • Note: while the Master Customer Financial File can't be read, renewals are rebuilt from HubSpot renewal deals. That version counts open renewals at full deal value, so GRR (P) and NRR (P) currently read high. A fix is in progress.

      Methodology and sources
      Projection
      • Retention by risk (open accounts): Low 100%, Medium 90%, High 75%, applied to the account's 2025 ARR. Risk is the renewal Risk level.
      • Projected 2026 ARR: booked renewals use the Master file Bookings actual (ARR Segment, finance-invoiced); open renewals use the unweighted CSM forecast × retention(risk). This changes PROJECTED ARR only - ACTUAL and RETAINED are unchanged.
      Formulas
      • GRR (P) [company]: (book gross retained + multi-year-locked 2025 ARR + partner ARR, all retained, capped at prior, no uplift) divided by the company 2025 software base (book + locked + partner). This is the company % gross renewed - the headline GRR.
      • % Gross Renewed (book): book gross retained divided by the book 2025 ARR - a book-subset retention rate, not the company GRR.
      • NRR (P): (sum of 2026 actual-or-projected over the book + multi-year-locked 2026 ARR + partner ARR + bookings upsell & expansion) divided by the 2025 base (book 2025 ARR + locked 2025 ARR + partner). NRR captures uplift/upsell; GRR does not.

        Methodology & sources
        • Open-pipeline weights: by pipeline stage, matching the Consero model — A. Engage 0%, B. Discovery 10%, C. Evaluation & Alignment 30% (POC 40%, not distinguishable in HubSpot), D. Selection & Scoping 60%, E. Agreement 90%, F. Booked / W 100%. Applied to every open deal across New, Upsell and Expansion. Deals project on CLOSE DATE: closes after 2026-12-31 are excluded from FY26 (BWX, UWV, OCLC today — flagged 27+). Closed bookings are stated at ARR-Segment software actuals (services stripped; the HubSpot deal is evidence, not the measure): closed New $123,965 · closed U/E $351,620 vs raw deal amounts $466,402.
        • Scope — 2026 close only: every headline number (closed and open, New and Upsell + Expansion) counts deals whose CLOSE DATE falls in 2026. Deals closing 2027+ remain listed in the deal tables for context but are excluded from all totals, counts and weighted figures.
        • Deal Score (X/7): the pipeline rubric (DP, W/U, WHY, ROI, P, D, C) parsed from core_customer_value, quality-aware. Most open deals score 0/7 because the markers are not yet populated, which is the intended gap signal.
        • Use Case: HubSpot primary_use_case is blank on open deals, so the column derives a short summary from each deal's value text; a dash means no usable text.
        • Sort: every deal table is ordered by close month. Open uses projected close; closed and closed-lost use actual.
        • Budget marker: dotted line on each bar at the anchors-shaped plan position for the reference date (2026-07-12), cumulative monthly plan through the prior month plus the elapsed fraction of the current month. The 2026 plan is back-loaded, so it reads low early. Exact value on hover.
        • J&J consolidation: 2 J&J Expansion deals at $25K shown as one "J&J Reference Data" line. Individual deals stay in HubSpot.
        • Flags: ⚠ name suggests renewal overlap (reconcile vs Renewals Book). 💰 amount may be multi-year TCV (Alstom $140K).

                              Bet by bet
                              Bet 1Strategic

                              We are the source of authoritative context when you can't get it wrong

                              Who owns context is already contested, with many vendors claiming it should live with them. Most infer context from the data they can reach. Inference is a fine way to get started, but some context is too costly to get wrong.

                              Processes that report to regulators, put safety or privacy at risk, or face risk and audit review need governed, authoritative context. That context is complex and must carry a clear audit trail. This bet is largely about doubling down on what we already do well.

                              What it means for the product
                              • Model complex context as a graph: definitions, relationships, policies and the rules that connect them.
                              • Enforce rules on context, so people and agents work only from what is valid and approved.
                              • Lead with governance: lifecycle, approvals, versioning and a full audit trail, deployable where regulated customers can validate it (SaaS or self-hosted, with long-term support releases).
                              • Ship authoritative content out of the box, such as industry and regulatory standards, so customers start from trusted context instead of a blank canvas.
                              Bet 2Strategic

                              Context can't live in a single vendor's ecosystem

                              Customers will want to own their context. Their use cases reach beyond the purpose-built context engines of hyperscalers and data warehouses, and no single platform sees the whole enterprise.

                              Once we capture complex context, we have to push it to where customers build and run agents. That means both serving it at runtime and binding it into the target systems.

                              What it means for the product
                              • Serve governed context to agents at runtime, through MCP and APIs, in whatever agent framework or LLM provider the customer chooses.
                              • Integrate governed context into the target systems customers use (data platforms, catalogs, BI tools, LLM context), in the form each one needs, with governance metadata and version history intact.
                              • Bind context to operational data where it lives, without copying the data or flattening the meaning.
                              Open questions
                              • Where does orchestration live: in the data platform, in an enterprise MCP gateway, or in the customer's own agent stack?
                              • Are we in the runtime path for every agent answer, or mostly at design time through what we compile into target systems?
                              • What trigger events show a customer that platform-native semantics have broken down?
                              • How do we prove the value (accuracy, reproducibility, token cost, audit trail) against a platform-native baseline?

                              Drawn from the Oct 2 architecture session and the Oct 5 positioning session.

                              Bet 3Strategic

                              The product experience becomes dramatically simpler

                              Users now expect a Claude- or ChatGPT-style experience: say what you want and it happens, without clicking through screens. Today, getting value from TopQuadrant often depends on our delivery team's expertise and judgment: scripts, queries and code to get data in, model it correctly and get it out.

                              What it means for the product
                              • Reimagine the experience around asking rather than navigating, with the focus on getting started and time to value.
                              • Codify our semantic expertise into AI skills and guided workflows, so customers can do on their own what PS does today.
                              • Start new users from the authoritative content in Bet 1, so building on their own is fast.
                              Bet 4Platform commitment

                              A platform as agile as AI-native entrants

                              This is a platform commitment, not a marketed bet. We will improve the codebase and infrastructure so that, over time, we can move as fast as new entrants and AI-native companies: shipping quickly, letting anyone start on their own, and running wherever customers need us.

                              What it means for the product
                              • A clear plan for scalability: know where the platform stops scaling and how we will address each limit.
                              • Automated deployment and releases, so shipping is routine and doesn't depend on a few people.
                              • Product-led adoption: a real way for anyone to start for free and reach value without a sales or PS engagement.
                              • One product that deploys on-prem and in SaaS without severe complication.
                              Proposed "not building" list
                              • A general-purpose chat or agent platform. Customers bring their own agents; our conversational experience (Bet 3) is for building and governing context.
                              • A text-to-SQL competitor to Genie.
                              • New Workato-based connectors.
                              • A pre-built connector for every request. Build the integration layer instead.
